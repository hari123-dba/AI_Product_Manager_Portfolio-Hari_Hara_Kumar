## Key takeaway
> A job statement that names a solution has already stopped being a job statement. My first draft said "I want to search with AI" — the honest version is "I want to stop losing the thread of what I was doing," and those two sentences point at completely different products.

## The three jobs

1. **Functional-dominant —** When I'm mid-way through a task and hit a term or claim I can't evaluate, I want to reach a usable answer and its sources in one pass, so I can keep working instead of opening six tabs and losing the thread.
2. **Emotional-dominant —** When I'm about to repeat something I learned from an AI answer to someone who knows the subject better than I do, I want to know which parts I can stand behind, so I can speak up without the quiet fear of being confidently wrong in public.
3. **Social-dominant —** When a colleague asks me where something stands and expects an answer today, I want to hand over something sourced that survives their spot-check, so I'm read as the person who brings evidence rather than opinions.
