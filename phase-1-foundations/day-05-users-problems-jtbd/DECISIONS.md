## D5.1 — Subject app: Perplexity

**Decision.** Perplexity is the app I wrote all three job statements against, and it is also my provisional Day 10–12 consumer teardown subject.

**Alternatives considered.**

| Option | Why it was tempting | Why I rejected it |
|---|---|---|
| A general chat assistant | The most-used AI product I have; endless review volume | Too many jobs at once. Three job statements against a general-purpose tool would each describe a different product, which teaches me nothing about focus. |
| A B2B coding copilot | Closest to my engineering background, easy to reason about | Week 3 already assigns a B2B teardown. Spending Day 5 there duplicates that and leaves the consumer side unexamined. |
| A note-taking app with AI features | Genuinely daily use | The AI is a side feature. The interesting product decisions are not AI-layer ones, so the Day 5 AI-fit analysis would be thin. |

**Why this one, against the five selection criteria.**

1. *I use it daily* — 10 sessions logged in 6 days, so my evidence is observed rather than recalled.
2. *Public review surface* — enough recent and critical store reviews and community threads to source real quotes.
3. *AI is the core, not a garnish* — the product's central bet is a model-layer one, so the AI-fit questions actually bite.
4. *Clear competing alternatives* — plain search and asking a colleague are strong, cheap, non-AI substitutes, which makes the "why AI" question honest rather than rhetorical.
5. *Reusable downstream* — the same subject carries Days 10–12, so the job map is built once and used three times.

**What would change my mind.** If, during Day 10 research, the public evidence turns out to be dominated by generic praise with no substantive complaint pattern, I will switch the teardown subject rather than write a teardown with nothing to analyse — the JTBD work here stays valid either way, since it rests on my own observed sessions. I will make that call by end of Day 10, not later, because Day 11 depends on it.

## D5.2 — Which three jobs to write

**Decision.** One functional-dominant, one emotional-dominant, one social-dominant job, rather than the three strongest jobs.

**Alternative considered.** Writing the three most frequent jobs — all of which, when I listed them, turned out to be functional variations on "answer my question faster."

**Why.** Three functional jobs produce one roadmap written three times. The dimensional spread is what exposes that Job 1 points at retrieval and context, Job 2 at verification UX, and Job 3 at tone and export — three different teams, three different metrics. A team that only wrote Job 1 would optimise answer speed while losing users to a trust failure it never measured.

**What would change my mind.** Evidence that the emotional and social jobs are rare in the real user base rather than merely rare in my own usage. I have one user here — me — and that is this document's main weakness.

## D5.3 — Evidence depth: stopping at 10 sessions and 12 public sources

**Decision.** Stopped collecting at 10 own sessions, 8 review quotes and 4 threads, inside the 90-minute build block.

**Alternative considered.** Extending into the write block to reach 20+ reviews.

**Why.** The quote pool had stopped producing new pain categories by roughly the sixth review — the remaining ones repeated themes I had already logged. Additional volume would have raised confidence in proportions I am not entitled to claim anyway, since this is not a representative sample and nothing here is tagged `[measured]`.

**What would change my mind.** If Day 10 research shows a pain category I never saw today, that is a signal my stopping rule was too aggressive, and I will raise the floor for the B2B teardown in Week 3.
